# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Technical decision-makers evaluating a development partner: CTOs, founders, and product leads at FinTech, HealthTech, e-commerce, and SaaS companies. They arrive skeptical of agency fluff and can smell a template. Their context: desk research, comparing vendors, deciding who to trust with a build. The job to be done is to confirm fanaticCoders has real engineering depth and ship-quality craft, fast enough to justify a conversation.

## Product Purpose

A marketing site for fanaticCoders, a digital agency selling full-stack development, web design, e-commerce, digital branding and marketing, SEO/PPC, and open-source work. It exists to convert technical buyers into project inquiries. Success means a credible visitor starts a project conversation. The site's own craft is the primary proof of competence: the site is the portfolio.

## Brand Personality

Technical and cutting-edge. A developer-native voice that uses code syntax as design language: npm-style commands, function-named CTAs like `startProject`, terminal metaphors. Three words: expert, precise, modern. Confident without hype; the polish does the talking. It should feel like it was built by senior engineers who care about detail.

## Anti-references

- **Generic template agencies.** Wix/Squarespace cookie-cutter layouts, stock-photo heroes, lorem-ipsum filler, interchangeable feature grids.
- **Corporate and stuffy.** Stiff enterprise-consulting energy, navy-suit formality, IBM/Accenture sterility.
- **Crypto/neon hype.** Neon-on-black glow overload, web3 buzzword aesthetic, hype over substance. (Tension to watch: the aurora palette and glass must stay tasteful and never tip into this.)
- **Cluttered marketplaces.** Dense, ad-heavy, noisy Fiverr/Upwork visual chaos.

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the proof.** Craft, performance, and motion quality ARE the sales pitch. Any rough edge undercuts the entire value proposition.
2. **Code as native language, not costume.** Terminal and code motifs must read as authentic developer fluency, never as a decorative gimmick.
3. **Restraint over hype.** The aurora gradients and glass are a seasoning, not the meal. Push toward tasteful and precise; pull back before crypto-neon.
4. **Show, don't claim.** Prefer demonstrated metrics, real work, and live interaction over adjective-stacked marketing copy.
5. **Earn the scroll.** Each section justifies its space; no filler, no restated headings.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA: sufficient contrast (audit gradient and glass text against tinted dark backgrounds), full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, alt text on imagery, semantic landmarks. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for the heavy GSAP and aurora animation so motion-sensitive users get a static, legible experience.
